PreambleThe Spirit Behind the Rules

These rules are framed to uphold fairness, clarity and a genuine level playing field for all participants. They are intended not as restrictions but as a structure that lets the game be contested in the right spirit, where preparation, skill and collective effort decide success.

By keeping uniform standards and transparent guidelines, the framework ensures balanced competition and meaningful on-field involvement, encouraging teams to perform as a unit rather than rely on a few individuals. It preserves the integrity of the tournament while celebrating participation, responsibility and the true spirit of cricket.

Article 2Shanti Devi Players Index (SPI) and Player Categories

Purpose

2.1
Every approved player receives an SPI score from 0 to 1000, built from their batting, bowling and fielding records. Each player then receives two ranks: an overall rank across the whole pool and an age-bracket rank against players of their own age bracket. Player categories follow from the bracket and the bracket rank.

Data sources

2.2
The SPI is built from:
SourceWhat is takenUse
CricHeroes career profile (the player’s own link)Matches, runs, batting average, strike rate, 4s and 6s, 50s, wickets, economy, bowling average, catches, stumpingsMain input
Registration formDate of birth, declared role, wicket-keeperAge bracket, role tie-break, keeper flag
2.3
Figures are read on the freeze date, 29 November 2026. Matches played after that date do not count. The League reads CricHeroes figures from each player’s own link.

Small samples

2.4
A record is a small sample when it is below 30% of the pool median: fewer than 30% of the median player’s matches for batting, or 30% of the median player’s wickets for bowling. A small-sample skill receives a standard low score of 10 out of 100 instead of its percentile.
2.5
The cut-off uses the median because a few very long careers pull the average up, and it moves with each season’s pool. On the league’s 1,000-player database at 3 November 2026 it falls at 101 matches (89 players below, 9%) and 56 wickets (205 players below, 21%).
2.6
A player with no record is Unrated, is placed in Category B, and may be moved by the grading panel after a trial or other evidence.

Skill scores

2.7
Each figure is converted to a percentile within the whole pool (0 = lowest, 100 = highest). For economy and bowling average, lower is better, so the order is reversed.
ScoreBuilt fromWeights
Batting (Bat)Batting average · strike rate · boundary %40% · 30% · 30%
Bowling (Bowl)Wickets per match · economy · bowling average40% · 30% · 30%
Fielding (Fld)Catches per match, plus stumpings for keepers100%
2.8
Boundary % is the share of balls faced hit for four or six: (4s + 6s) ÷ balls faced × 100, where balls faced = runs ÷ strike rate × 100.

Role

2.9
Each player’s role is set from the two main scores:
RoleRule
All-rounderBat ≥ 60 and Bowl ≥ 60 (top 40% of the pool in both)
BatterNot an all-rounder, and Bat ≥ Bowl
BowlerNot an all-rounder, and Bowl > Bat
Wicket-keeperA flag added to the role when the player keeps
Where the two scores are within 2 points, the role declared on registration decides.

The SPI score

2.10
The SPI score is calculated as:
SPI = 10 × ( 0.70 × max(Bat, Bowl) + 0.20 × min(Bat, Bowl) + 0.10 × Fld )
Ties are split by career matches (more first), then by registration date (earlier first).

Ranks and age brackets

2.11
A player’s age is their completed years on Sunday 7 March 2027, the day of the last league match. The age brackets are Under 35, 35–39 and 40 and over. Only players aged 30 or above on that date may take part.
2.12
Each player receives an overall rank (among every approved player), an age-bracket rank (among players of the same bracket), and a place in the bracket’s role lists: top batters by Bat, top bowlers by Bowl, and all-rounders by SPI.

Categories

2.13
Every player under 35 is Category A+, whatever their score. They still receive both ranks.
2.14
In the 35–39 and 40-and-over brackets, the top players by age-bracket rank are Category A and the rest are Category B. Each bracket is ranked only against itself.
2.15
The number of Category A places is enough for 14 teams to hold up to 6 each, with some choice left, shared between the two brackets in proportion to the number of players in each, and never more than half a bracket:
A places = 14 teams × 6 × 1.25 = 105 bracket A = 105 × (players in bracket ÷ players in both brackets)
2.16
Within each bracket, the A places are shared in proportion to the bracket’s mix of batters, bowlers and all-rounders, each share filled from the top of its role list; places left over go to the next-best SPI.
2.17
A player is placed in the Legend category, instead of A+, A or B, if they are 60 or older on the cut-off date, or their career batting average is below 10 and their career strike rate is below 100 (raw career figures). The Board may also name any player a Legend, or make an exception, on a reasonable basis that it records. A Legend keeps both SPI ranks and is left out of the Category A count.

Worked example

Governance

2.19
A grading panel of at least three people runs the index and signs off the categories.
2.20
The panel may move a player by one category, with written reasons, where the record clearly misleads, for example after a long injury or with a thin, very old record.
2.21
A player may ask once for a review before the auction pool is published. The panel’s decision after review is final.
2.22
Scores, ranks and categories are frozen when the auction pool is published. The weights and thresholds in this Article are reviewed after each season.
2.23
Players are admitted to the auction pool only after a refinement process that ensures only eligible corporate players are considered; professional players are excluded.
2.24
Owners are included in the SPI ranking, so that teams have a complete view of the pool.
2.25
The League will explain the ranking of any individual player when an owner asks.

Reporting requirements. At the reporting time each team must confirm its playing XII, submit the official team sheet, and ensure all its players are present in the approved team kit. 4.8
Match duration. A match is expected to take about 3 hours 30 minutes, including the mid-innings break. Each innings has a maximum of 100 minutes, within which the bowling side must complete its 20 overs. Failing to keep the over rate may lead to fielding restrictions or other penalties decided by the match officials.

Article 5Tournament Format and Qualification

5.1
Fourteen (14) teams take part, divided into Group A and Group B of seven teams each. Group allocation is decided by lottery.
5.2
Each team plays six (6) league matches.
5.3
The top two teams of each group qualify for the Semi-Finals:
  • Semi-Final 1: Group A Rank 1 v Group B Rank 2
  • Semi-Final 2: Group B Rank 1 v Group A Rank 2
5.4
The winners of the Semi-Finals play the Final.
5.5
The points table follows standard T20 league scoring unless announced otherwise: Win = 2 points, No Result = 1 point.
5.6
Where teams are level on points, they are separated by Net Run Rate, then head-to-head result, then higher runs scored, as the organisers decide.
5.7
A loss earns 0 points. A No Result (a match that cannot be completed) earns 1 point for each team.

6.2

6.3
The playing XII may include at most one (1) player under 35 and at most four (4) players aged 35–39. There is no limit on players aged 40 and over. Ages are taken on the cut-off date in clause 2.11.
6.4
Owner players. A team may have up to three (3) playing owners: at most two (2) in Category A, who must be aged 40 or over, and at most one (1) in Category B, who may be aged 35 or over. Owner players count towards the 18 and all limits above.
6.5
Retention. A team may retain two (2) players who played in Season 1: one (1) Category A and one (1) Category B, both aged 40 or over. No player under 35 may be retained, and Category A+ players may be acquired only at the auction.

Any change to a squad after the registration deadline requires organiser approval.
6.8
A team may make at most three (3) replacements, from the unsold auction pool only, each from the same age bracket and the same or a lower category. No outside players are permitted.
6.9
Minimum squad. Each team must complete a squad of at least sixteen (16) players at the auction, including owner players and Legends. Non-playing owners do not count towards this minimum.
6.10
A team that fails to reach the minimum squad is penalised: its most expensive purchase is released back into the auction pool, and fifty percent (50%) of that player’s purchase value is forfeited and deducted from the team’s purse.
6.11
Of a team’s three owner players, not all may be Category A; at least one must be Category B in the final SPI-based categorisation. Owners are classified strictly under the SPI and the owner conditions in this Article. Any deviation or special consideration for an owner arises only through prior discussion and agreement between the owner and the League Director at the time of onboarding, is taken solely to keep the competition balanced and fair, and the League Director’s decision is final and binding.
6.12
Any eleven (11) players from the playing XII may bat, and any eleven (11) may bowl. The wicketkeeper must be one of the playing XII. Other squad players may be used only as fielding substitutes.

Article 10Standard T20 Playing Rules

10.1
Each side bats for twenty (20) overs.
10.2
The standard T20 powerplay applies: the first five (5) overs, plus one (1) over between overs 11 and 15, with fielding restrictions.
10.3
A bowler may bowl at most four (4) overs.
10.4
Each team receives two (2) DRS reviews to challenge the umpire’s decision in both innings.
10.5
If a knockout match is tied, a Super Over may be played.
10.6
Net Run Rate for the league standings is calculated by the standard method in the CricHeroes app.
10.7
Umpire and match referee decisions are final on the field.
10.8
Powerplay detail. Powerplay 1 is the first five overs of each innings. Powerplay 2 is one over that the batting team may take between overs 11 and 15; if it is not taken earlier, the 15th over automatically becomes Powerplay 2. Only two fielders are allowed outside the 30-yard circle during either powerplay.
10.9
Over rate. Teams must complete their 20 overs within the allotted time. If the bowling side falls behind the required over rate, the number of fielders allowed outside the 30-yard circle may be reduced for the remaining overs.
10.10
DRS reviews may be used for LBW, run-out and stumping decisions.
10.11
Rain and interruptions. If a match is interrupted by weather or unavoidable circumstances, the Duckworth-Lewis-Stern (DLS) method applies and the match officials set the revised target or overs. In exceptional cases the organiser may reschedule or replay the match.

Article 14Code of Conduct and Discipline

The Shanti Devi Legends League is built on sportsmanship, respect and gentlemanly conduct. All players and captains are expected to uphold the dignity of the League during matches.

14.1
Spirit of the game. Cricket must always be played in the true spirit of the game. Players must respect opponents, umpires, teammates and match officials at all times. The League values healthy competition, camaraderie and enjoyment over aggression or confrontation.
14.2
Respect for umpires. Umpire decisions are final and binding. Players must not argue, protest or show dissent. Only the captain may politely approach the umpire for clarification, and only between deliveries or overs. Aggressive behaviour towards umpires invites immediate disciplinary action.
14.3
Behaviour on the field. Players must not use abusive or offensive language, sledge or make personal remarks, or engage in physical confrontation. Players should communicate positively, encourage teammates and accept success and failure with dignity.
14.4
Captains. Captains hold special responsibility for discipline. They must ensure their team follows league rules, control disputes involving their players, communicate respectfully with umpires and officials, and prevent situations from escalating. A captain may be held accountable if their team repeatedly breaks conduct rules.
14.5
Opponents. Players must treat opponents with respect and courtesy, avoid provoking or insulting gestures, and shake hands and acknowledge good performances.
14.6
Safety and fair play. Players must avoid dangerous or reckless play, respect the fielding safety rules, and tell the umpire if they believe a player is injured or unfit to continue.
14.7
Time discipline. Teams must be ready on the field on time and avoid unnecessary delays between overs or innings.
14.8
Integrity. Misrepresenting player information or a CricHeroes profile, falsely declaring or concealing a professional cricketing background to gain an advantage, manipulating league processes, or concealing facts that may affect the fairness of the competition breaks the trust the League operates on and invites strict disciplinary action.
14.9
Social media. Players must not post abusive or defamatory comments about the League, players or officials, or share misleading or negative content that damages the League’s reputation. Constructive feedback should go directly to the organisers.
14.10
Disciplinary action. Breaking this Code may lead to an official warning, penalty runs to the opposition, temporary suspension from the match, a match ban, or League suspension in serious cases. The League Organising Committee’s decision is final.
14.11
Commitment. By taking part, every player and captain agrees to respect the spirit of the League, promote friendship and community among players, and represent their team and the League with dignity and sportsmanship.
14.12
Verification and objection window. Category A+ and A players have been vetted by the organisers as far as practicable. Category B players may not have had the same depth of verification, and in some cases exceptions may have been granted (including, but not limited to, the absence of a LinkedIn profile or age-related considerations) based on SPI rankings and the information available. Because complete verification of a player’s cricketing history or occupation is not always feasible:
  • team owners may raise objections about any player’s eligibility, background or possible misrepresentation;
  • an objection must be raised formally before the start of that player’s team’s second scheduled match;
  • if no objection is raised in time and the player plays the second match, the player is deemed fully cleared for the rest of the league, and no later objection, dispute or penalty about that player’s eligibility is entertained.
14.13
All player and owner profiles are self-declared and must comply strictly with league rules. A false declaration or suppression of material information found within the objection window may lead to disciplinary action under league rules.
